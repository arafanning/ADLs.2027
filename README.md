# Cannabis use, everyday functioning, and medication adherence among people living with HIV

**Dissertation study 3 · Manuscript in preparation**

*Working study title:* Past-year cannabis use relates to medication adherence but not broader everyday functioning among people living with HIV.

## Research context

Neurocognitive performance does not consistently translate into difficulty with daily responsibilities. Someone's performance on a structured medication-management task, for example, does not establish how consistently they take prescribed medication outside the assessment setting. This study therefore examines everyday functioning through several distinct outcomes rather than treating them as interchangeable measures of the same ability.

Among people living with HIV, cannabis-use groups were not significantly associated with reported decline or current difficulty in activities of daily living, or with performance-based medication-management capacity, after the examined adjustments. The clearer association involved **self-reported medication nonadherence**, which differed across levels of past-year cannabis use. Depressive symptoms also warrant attention in the interpretation of ADL difficulties. These findings are observational and do not establish that cannabis use causes medication nonadherence.

This is the **final study comprising my dissertation**. An accepted poster on this work is scheduled for the 2027 International Neuropsychological Society meeting. Research on inflammatory biomarkers may be pursued as a **separate publication after the dissertation defense**, but is not a fourth dissertation study.

## Repository contents and limitations

The repository currently contains **working R scripts** for setup, data preparation, descriptive analyses, and exploration of several functional outcomes, along with a `Figures` file. Some scripts contain substantial exploratory material or repeated analysis steps and should not be interpreted as a single, finalized pipeline.

Participant-level data are **restricted and not publicly shared**. The repository is provided for documentation and method development, not as a validated, independently reproducible analysis package. Manuscript and presentation versions may differ as analyses and reporting are finalized.
